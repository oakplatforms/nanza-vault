## 1. Cap table & equity baseline

*Confirms the starting point before any outside money is layered in.*

### Q1. Is the current equity split (45/45/10) between us finalized and documented, including a vesting schedule?

**Answer:** The 45/45/10 split is documented in our incorporation filing, but I would not call it final. It reflects the initial agreement Tahir and I reached at formation: 10% reserved for early contributors and pre-seed investment, with the understanding that we would formalize the structure, through a SAFE or a priced round, as the business matured. There is no vesting schedule in place today, and the split remains adjustable. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q2. Do we have a founders' agreement in place covering IP assignment, vesting, and what happens if one of us leaves?

**Answer:** No. We do not yet have a founders' agreement covering IP assignment, role definitions, vesting, or departure terms. Vesting in particular is a gap we need to close. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q3. Are there any other people (early employees, advisors, contractors) who believe they're owed equity that isn't yet documented?

**Answer:** Nothing is in writing, but three people have early involvement that should be reflected in the structure. Gino has advised us informally for roughly six months, predating anyone else, and we intend to work with him long term; as his role formalizes, his compensation should reflect that early contribution. Jillian and Nick joined as consultants under the 90-day plan and may convert to early employees under a similar model: advisory involvement that becomes a defined role if the company scales. None of this is documented today.

One principle behind all of this: equity is not only a financial stake we grant, it is also a reflection of how much someone loves and is invested in the business. We want involvement to scale along a ladder, from introduction and onboarding, to early project development and product ownership, to equity partnership, based on how deeply a person is committed to what we are building. We are equally comfortable compensating people at a contribution or consulting level, which will be the right fit for many. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q4. Have we set aside an option pool for future hires, and if so, how large should it be before our pre-seed closes?

**Answer:** Not yet, and I am open to recommendations on size. An option pool is a given for a technology startup; equity is part of the compensation for the work it takes to make this succeed, so early compensation will be a mix of cash and equity. The open questions before the pre-seed close are how large the pool needs to be and what cash-to-equity mix we offer. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

## 2. Raise amount & use of funds

*Validates the $250K–$750K range and what it actually needs to accomplish.*

### Q5. What is our actual monthly burn rate today, and how does that change once we make our first 1–2 hires?

**Answer:** Current burn is minimal: roughly $500 a month for AWS development infrastructure and AI tooling such as Claude, plus about $100 in other expenses. That is exceptionally low for the scale of product we have built, and we could reduce it to around $300 by shifting to smaller open source AI models. That efficiency is how we reached this point, not how we intend to grow. With funding, burn rises sharply because salaries and marketing spend arrive together. I cannot give a precise figure yet, but the order of magnitude is roughly $25,000 a month once we invest in marketing and compensate additional people. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q6. At the low end ($250K) versus the high end ($750K) of our range, how many months of runway does each buy us?

**Answer:** Using a planning figure of $25K a month, $250K buys about 10 months of runway and $750K about 30 months. That level funds a real marketing budget and meaningful compensation for the initial team. Because the team is close-knit and invested in the company, we can also weight early contributors toward equity over cash, which pulls burn toward $15–20K. Realistically the range is $15–25K a month; even at the high end of $25K this is a very lean operation that still preserves a solid marketing budget. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q7. What specific, measurable milestone (first paying customer, $X MRR, a retention number) do we need to hit before this money runs out?

**Answer:** Traditional pre-seed milestones, first paying customer and a retention figure, still apply and we will report both. But the product and its go-to-market production system are already built, largely due to advances in AI, so this capital should carry us well past first customers. The target is penetration of multiple trading card game communities, successful launches in each, and thousands of users across multiple games, particularly at the $750K end. The milestone is weighted toward growth, not build. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q8. If we raise smaller and stack multiple small checks, are we both comfortable with the extra dilution and negotiation overhead that entails?

**Answer:** Multiple checks, yes. Multiple raises, no. A $750K pre-seed can reasonably combine friends, family, and several investors into a single round. What I want to avoid is three sequential raises of roughly $250K each. That approach fatigues the team and complicates negotiation and paperwork, because terms shift as the business advances; investors coming in on a third raise would be on materially different terms from the first. One round, many checks. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q9. Do we agree on the split between funding hires versus funding customer acquisition, or does that need more discussion?

**Answer:** The exact split is not set and will develop with the plan, but the direction is clear. We will fund some hires and want to compensate contributors, while relying heavily on automation and AI agents and scaling with AI as far as it goes. That makes this a three-way allocation: AI agents and token usage, human workforce, and customer acquisition. Because the product is already built, deploying capital into customer acquisition is our biggest advantage over other companies raising today and the most compelling story for an investor. We should weight spend toward customer acquisition, staff through a contributor model backed by AI, and treat getting customers into the system as the top priority with the investment. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

## 3. Valuation cap & SAFE terms

*Ensures both cofounders agree on the negotiating position before talking to investors.*

### Q10. Are we both aligned on using a SAFE (rather than a priced round or convertible note) for this raise?

**Answer:** Yes. A SAFE is the right instrument for this raise. Tahir's confirmation is still pending. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q11. What valuation cap are we comfortable opening negotiations at, and what's our walk-away floor?

**Answer:** My opening frame is an $8–10M valuation, giving up roughly 10% for the full $750K. Since the cap determines how much equity that money buys, the real question is what valuation makes $750K equal 10%. The math: $750K for 10% implies a $7.5M post-money cap (about $6.75M pre-money). At an $8M post-money cap, $750K buys 9.4%; at $10M, 7.5%. So an $8–10M post-money cap is consistent with the 10% target at the high end of the raise. The walk-away floor is not yet set. *(Answered 2026-09-24; needs comparable-cap research before it's final.)*

### Q12. Would we consider a discount-only raise? If so, what rate?

**Answer:** Yes. Jillian walked me through discount-only SAFEs and I am comfortable with that structure if it is what early investors prefer. Rate not yet chosen. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q13. Will we offer a discount rate in addition to a cap, and if so, what rate?

**Answer:** Open to it. I understand the difference between cap-based and discount-based terms and am comfortable with a cap, a discount, or both, depending on investor preference. Rate not yet chosen. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q14. How do we plan to handle stacking SAFEs from multiple small investors at potentially different caps?

**Answer:** We are open to different caps if that is what it takes to bring the right investors in, but the priority is keeping this to a single pre-seed round. The cap in question is the SAFE's valuation cap, the maximum company valuation at which an investor's money converts to equity in the next priced round. The common pattern is an early anchor investor getting a lower cap than later investors in the same round, and I am comfortable with that as long as the differences are small, written down, and everyone is on the same SAFE template with the same optional discount. What I want to avoid is raising several times and letting the valuation shift between raises, which fatigues the team and complicates the cap table. If a later raise is needed we can address it then; for now the plan is one pre-seed round, open to small and large investors alike, even if not every check is at exactly the same cap. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q15. Who has final say if we disagree on accepting a specific investor's terms?

**Answer:** Tahir and I, together. We would need to agree on a given set of terms based on where the company stands at the time, advised by Jillian, Gino, and Nick, with particular weight on Jillian given her direct role in investor relations. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

## 5. Traction, metrics & the pitch narrative

*Validates that both cofounders tell the same story about where the company stands.*

### Q21. What are the 3–5 metrics we will report consistently to investors, and do we both track them the same way today?

**Answer:** Four metrics, in order of weight.

1. **New users.**
2. **Transaction volume.** This is central to why people leave Discord. Today no one knows how much trading happens in Facebook groups and DMs; it is a black hole, and peer-to-peer activity could represent 10, 20, or 30% of the market. Our transaction data gives real insight into that, and it supports one of our core theses: bringing structure to zero-commission trades. Investors will want this number.
3. **Retention / return frequency.** How often users come back, whether measured as retention or overall usage.
4. **Tagging volume and trending tags.** The volume of tagging and the ability to surface trending games and what a community is actually discussing. Being able to say "this is exactly what this community is talking about right now" is a strong investor story.

Whether Tahir and I currently track these the same way is not yet confirmed. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q22. Why hasn't revenue started yet, and can we both explain that gap in the same terms to a skeptical investor?

**Answer:** Because we are deliberately in the community-development stage, and the model we are following says that is the correct order. Discord spent its first year building communities and launched Nitro, now its primary revenue source at roughly $545M a year, in year two. Our caps-and-credits model has the same shape: join free, use it free, and once you are genuinely engaged, contributing content, data, insights, and conversation, you pay a small monthly fee that feels like giving back to something you value. Based on our Discord research it is a very sticky model. The honest framing for a skeptical investor is that we do not want revenue in the first six months; community comes first and revenue grows out of usage. We carry the expenses for that window, which are minor and should not rise much as infrastructure use grows. That period lets us onboard communities and earn their loyalty, at which point the fee reads as a minor cost for something they are deeply invested in. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q23. What does "success" at the end of this raise look like, in specific numbers, that we both agree on?

**Answer:** Two outcomes. First, the right investors: people who are passionate about the space or genuinely want to join it, who are smart and engaged, who have our interests at heart as a Black-founded company, and who buy the full vision of Nanza as a company beyond Flesh and Blood and beyond trading cards. Second, a raise near the $750K end of the range, so we can fund all three pillars: AI, marketing, and growing the human side of the team. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q24. Are there any product, team, or market risks we should proactively disclose rather than let an investor discover in diligence?

**Answer:** Largely no. Team: nothing I am aware of. Product: the model is peer-to-peer, zero-commission trading, and we have done the legal work to confirm it is permissible in all 50 states, so there is nothing to disclose. Market: the collectibles space is volatile. Games, especially major IPs, spike at launch and then struggle to sustain a strong community; that takes time. Those risks are real, but we understand them, they are inherent to trading cards and collectibles broadly, and our platform is designed to smooth exactly those inconsistencies. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q25. Who will own writing and sending investor updates once money starts coming in?

**Answer:** Jillian leads, with the whole team supporting. We operate on a swarm model: wherever someone is producing results, we concentrate enthusiasm, momentum, human and AI resources, and strategy behind them so the success compounds. Investor updates work the same way. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

**Answer:** As a team, with Jillian leading on anything investor-related. Tahir and I try to reach agreement first, drawing on Jillian's guidance throughout. If the two of us still cannot agree with her advisory input, the decision goes to the full team (Jillian, Gino, and Nick) to break the tie. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*

### Q29. What information rights (financials, cap table access, regular updates) are we comfortable committing to contractually?

I have seen investors that deep in a company, and it is not the dynamic we want. *(Answered 2026-09-24.)*
