## Part 1: The pitch

### The one-liner

**Nanza is the social platform for trading card communities.** Players and collectors post, follow the games and cards they love, and buy, sell, and trade with people they trust. Trading carries no commission. The product is free, and members pay to raise their limits, the same model as Discord.

Longer version: *We give card communities the infrastructure they're missing.* It's built for collectors and players, and for the stores, leagues, creators, and groups that hold the hobby together.

### The problem: card communities trade in the dark

We came to this as collectors. Then we looked at the whole community around the cards, and here's what we saw:

1. **Communities run their own marketplaces.** The real trading happens in Facebook groups, Discord servers, Reddit, and DMs. The big marketplaces take too much in commission, and they're catalogs, with no conversation.
2. **Trust runs on screenshots.** Community marketplaces have no reviews and no ratings. Buyers and sellers check every deal by hand, with screenshots and references.
3. **There are no standards for product data.** Third-party marketplaces mix up publisher artwork, listing photos, and brand assets.
4. **Brand integrity breaks down.** The further a card gets from its publisher, the less coherent the brand gets. The ecosystem is active but has no structure.

Nobody knows how much trading happens in Facebook groups and DMs. It could be 10%, 20%, or 30% of the market. Today that volume can't be measured, and nobody earns from it in a structured way.

### The market

The TCG world is bigger and more mobile than the tools that serve it.

| Figure | Source |
| --- | --- |
| **$13B** global trading cards market in 2024, growing about 8.5% a year through 2034 | Market Decipher / PRNewswire, Jan 2025 |
| **15.3M** cards sent to PSA for grading in 2024, up 16% year over year | Mordor Intelligence, 2025 |
| **About 36%** of physical TCG collectors actively trade in secondary markets and at events | Business Research Insights, 2026 |

Collectors are mobile and care about condition. More and more of them treat cards as both a hobby and a soft asset class. The category is mature enough to deserve infrastructure built for it.

### The product

Nanza is mobile-first (iOS and Android) and live in production in the US today, starting with Flesh and Blood.

- **Social feed.** Follow heroes, classes, sets, and cards, and your feed fills with the posts, listings, and people around them. Trending tags show what a community is talking about right now.
- **Trade with 0% commission.** Connect with someone, check their reviews, and message them before you make a deal. This keeps the friend-to-friend trading people already do on Discord, and adds trust and structure to it.
- **A marketplace that speaks card.** Condition grading, printings, treatments, alt arts, and bulk buying and listing are all built in. Every listing carries the details a collector checks before buying.
- **Collections and card scanning.** Scan or search to build a collection. Every card is linked to live pricing, conversation, and activity.
- **Groups.** Private, local, and niche spaces for each game, built to improve on or replace the marketplaces running on Discord and Facebook today. Sellers can post publicly, locally, or both.
- **One source of truth.** Canonical artwork, metadata, and structure stay the same everywhere. Product images and listing photos are never mixed up. We saw early how much IP matters in this space, so data integrity has been a priority from the start.

**The loop we close.** Today discovery, listings, conversation, and trust live in separate places, with broken handoffs. Nanza puts the whole lifecycle of a card in one place.

### Business model: community first, then membership

- **Free tier, like Discord.** Posting, card scanning, and trading cost nothing.
- **Premium membership.** Members who want higher limits upgrade. Hitting the cap doesn't lock anyone out, because the free version keeps working with fewer features. This is the main revenue line.
- **Premium content (Q1 2027).** Creators put content behind a paywall inside Nanza and get paid in the app.
- **Vendor commerce (December 2026).** Vendors list on Nanza with 0% commission. When a sale comes through a creator's post, it carries a 5% referral commission.
- **Vendor technology services (2027).** White-label sites and apps, AI-assisted publishing tools, and cloud support for vendors growing globally.

**Why there's no revenue yet.** We're deliberately in the community-building stage. Discord spent its first year building communities and launched Nitro in year two. Nitro is now its main revenue source, at about $545M a year. Our plan has the same shape: people join free, use it free, and pay a small monthly fee once they're truly invested. For the first six months, community comes first.

### Go-to-market: enter one community at a time, through the people it already trusts

Our playbook, proven on Flesh and Blood, has three parts:

1. **Relationships first.** We get to know a game's publisher before we build in its community, so our work is never met with takedown notices. We have a working relationship with Legend Story Studios, the publisher of Flesh and Blood.
2. **Creators next.** The affiliate program brings in the voices the community already listens to. For Flesh and Blood that means eight creators across YouTube, podcasts, Patreon, and cosplay, including The Card Guyz, AggroBlaze96, LupineFiasco, BiosUpdate, Flesh and Pod, Pitch It To Me, The Threaded Ren, and HelpMeJace.
   - October pilot: $300 per affiliate for three posts, shared to their audience, plus honest product feedback.
   - Affiliate links (live October 22): a referral URL that pays up to $20 for each new user, in four $5 tiers (5 posts, 5 offers, 5 listings, 10 collection cards). It has a $15K budget across 10 affiliates, with a $5K cap per affiliate. Each affiliate also gets a custom landing page.
   - After the pilot budget is spent, affiliates earn through vendor referral commissions, premium content, and causes.
3. **Trusted vendors last.** We proposed that FAB Foundry become our primary vendor. They get top placement, 0% commission, and technology support. We get 1,000 listings to start and a partner to help shape our vendor tools. Other community vendors join in 2027. Independent sellers are live today.

Every community is different. Flesh and Blood lives on YouTube, and another game might live on TikTok. So each game gets its own strategy, built the way the affiliate program was.

### Expansion: from one game to many

**The pipeline.** A 90-day outreach campaign (October–December 2026) is underway to licensors, publishers, and founders across more than 50 games:

- **Licensor- and publisher-backed TCGs** (led by Skylar), about 35 games, including Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece, Lorcana, Star Wars: Unlimited, Riftbound, Digimon, Gundam, Dragon Ball, Weiss Schwarz, Final Fantasy, and more from Bandai, Bushiroad, Hasbro, Konami, Disney, Riot/UVS, and Upper Deck.
- **Founder-backed indie TCGs** (led by Gino), about 17 games, including Elestrals (about $1.5M Kickstarter), Neuroscape, Alpha Clash, Metazoo, and WONDRLND. These are grassroots games that could become the next Pokémon.
- **Living card games**, 4 games (Gudnak, Marvel Champions, Arkham Horror, Lord of the Rings). They're played between rounds at TCG events, so they fit communities we already serve.

The ask in every conversation is just a 30-minute call. We don't pitch a partnership up front. This approach has early signal: when we tested it, Asmodee CEO Thomas Koegler replied asking which opportunities we wanted to explore so he could route us to the right people.

**The platform work (done by January 1, led by Tahir).**

- Architecture and design for many separate game communities side by side.
- **AI migration tooling** that fetches and loads a new game's card data and prices, so adding a game is a repeatable process instead of a one-off project.
- **Cortex v1**, our pricing engine. It moves from proof of concept to production, built to fetch prices and power pricing insights across every game.

**Next communities (January–March 2027).** We'll choose the two or three games that fit best and enter each one the way we entered Flesh and Blood.

**Global.** Nanza is US-only today. The infrastructure is built to deploy across regions without a rewrite. The rollout to Canada, Europe, South America, and Asia, with multi-language support, begins in Q1 2027.

### Why us, why now

- **The product is already built.** With AI-heavy development, a small team shipped a full social marketplace (mobile, web, admin, payments, shipping, pricing) on about $600 a month in costs. Investor money goes to growth, not to building the product.
- **We're in the community.** The founders are collectors. Our go-to-market runs through creators the community already trusts, and we build relationships with each game's publisher before we enter, so we don't run into takedowns.
- **Data no one else has.** Peer-to-peer trading volume in Discord and Facebook groups can't be measured today. Nanza makes it visible, which gives collectors and communities a real view of what they value.
- **A model that's worked before.** Discord showed that free community plus a paid membership becomes a durable revenue source at scale.

### The raise

- **Pre-seed, $250K–$750K, on a SAFE.** One round with many checks, not several small raises one after another.
- **Use of funds, in three parts:** AI agents and tokens, a small human team (weighted toward equity), and customer acquisition, which gets the most.
- **Runway:** at $15–25K a month, $250K lasts about 10 months and $750K about 30.
- **Milestone:** launches in several TCG communities and thousands of users across multiple games, which is a growth target rather than a build target.
- **Metrics we'll report:** new users, transaction volume (including zero-commission trades), retention, and tagging and trending-tag volume.
- **Who we're looking for:** angels and specialist funds in marketplaces, gaming, tabletop, and consumer tech, who have real passion for cards and collectibles.

## Part 2: Conflicts and gaps in the source material

**Use the newest decks; the May decks are out of date.** The May 2026 decks (v1–v3) were written for Legend Story Studios, before the strategy changed. Where they disagree with the September–October decks, this overview follows the newer ones:

| Topic | May decks (v1–v3) | Current (Sep–Oct decks) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Games live | One Piece, Magic, Pokémon live; FaB "next" | **Flesh and Blood only**; multi-game by Jan 1 |
| Positioning | "Mobile-first marketplace" with revenue share, escrow, and authentication | **Social platform**, 0% commission trades, Discord-style membership |
| Audience | Pitch to the publisher (LSS) | Players and collectors are the customer; publisher relationships come first, then creators, then vendors |

**Placeholder numbers in v1 that must not reach investors.** v1's traction and market numbers are filler: 12k+ MAU, "$X.XM" GMV, 4.8★ rating, sub-2% disputes, 47-second listing time, 73% mobile, ~$3B North American market, plus several lorem ipsum lines. **The traction slide needs real figures** (users, posts, trades/offers, collection cards, affiliate-link signups) before this goes out.

**Placeholders in v2/v3.** "Founded 2024 · Backed by [investors]" and the contact names are unfilled. Confirm the founding year.

**Affiliate terms changed between decks.** The affiliates deck (Sep 18) describes links in November, a one-time payment on a referred user's first deal, and 5% a month of their premium membership. The onboarding deck (Oct 20) describes links live October 22 with four $5 tiers capped at $20 per user. This overview uses the October terms. Decide whether the 5% recurring membership share is still planned.

**Premium content timing.** The affiliates deck says January 2027 and the onboarding deck says Q1 2027. This overview uses Q1 2027.

**Commission vs. service fee.** Every deck says "0% commission on trades." The app also charges buyers a service fee on marketplace purchases (3% + $0.50, per the September 2026 decision). We need one clear sentence on that before an investor asks: trades are free, and purchases carry a buyer-paid service fee. That fee is also an early revenue line the decks don't mention.

**FAB Foundry status.** The vendor deck is a proposal (draft, Oct 6). Don't present FAB Foundry as signed until it is. The same goes for the affiliates: the deck calls them "prospective," so confirm who has actually signed before naming them.

**Asmodee.** As of the Sep 21 deck, the thread had a reply but no meeting booked. Update the status before citing it.

**Team roles.** Gino, Jillian, and Nick have no titles in the decks. Fill these in.
