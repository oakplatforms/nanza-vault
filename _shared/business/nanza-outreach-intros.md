# Nanza Outreach Intros

Standard first-touch introductions for the outreach campaign that starts **Monday 2026-10-05**. One consistent way to introduce Nanza to each audience, written for the channel we actually reach them on. These are introductions only — no full scripts. Everything after the first reply is handled as a team.

Companion docs: [[nanza-connection-map]] (who we already know, by bucket and company), [[nanza-onboarding-90-day]] (the two game tiers and the 90-day plan), and the internal `multi-game-support` deck in `nanza-decks` (the workstreams, leads, and the Asmodee test thread this is modeled on).

## Ground rules

1. **Any reply goes in Slack.** Investor, CEO, founder, department head, a second-tier contact — it doesn't matter. Before anyone answers a follow-up, post it in Slack and regroup. The intro is the only thing sent solo.
2. **One ask: thirty minutes.** No partnership pitch, no deck, no proposal in the first message. The proof point is that we're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios.
3. **Short and clean.** The base blurb below is about 70 words. Intros stay under that plus a two-sentence ask. Never send the internal deck.
4. **Professional, yet personal.** Do your research before you write. Know who they are, what they've built or backed, and what they care about, and let one specific, true detail show that.

## The base blurb

This is the paragraph that has already gone out and worked (it's what opened the Asmodee thread). Every variation below is built from it. Don't rewrite it per send; trim it.

> Nanza is a social platform built for TCGs. Players already buy, sell, and trade inside Discord servers and Facebook groups with no structure behind it. Nanza lets them keep trading with their friends and local groups, in a product designed around their needs. The business model is similar to Discord: the product is free and members pay to raise their limits. There is no commission on trades.

**Short form (for 300-character notes and DMs):**

> Nanza is a social platform for TCG players — the Discord and Facebook-group trading people already do, in a product built for it. Free to use, no commission on trades.

**Proof point (add to any version when there's room):**

> We're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios, so we can share real usage rather than a concept.

## Group 1 — Founder-led TCGs

Indie trading card games run by one to three founders, with no venture backing and usually no corporate licensor behind them. Most had a table at Gen Con 2026, and they're the games that could become the next big thing. We reach them wherever they already are: LinkedIn, Discord, X, Instagram, Bluesky, and email once we find it. The tone is informal, one builder to another, and every message shows we actually know their game. The ask is thirty minutes with the founder directly, no intermediaries.

### LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars)

> Hi [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza, a social platform for TCG players (free, no commission on trades). Big fan of what you're building with [Game]. I would love to connect and learn more about your community.

### LinkedIn / Discord / social DM

> Hey [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza. We're building a social platform for TCG players: the buying, selling, and trading people already do in Discord servers and Facebook groups, in a product designed for it. Free to use, no commission on trades. We're live with Flesh and Blood today and looking at which games to bring on next year.
>
> I've been following [Game] since [Gen Con / the Kickstarter / specific thing], and I would love thirty minutes to learn more about your community.

### Email

**Subject:** Nanza × [Game] — quick intro

> Hi [Recipient name],
>
> I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza. Nanza is a social platform built for TCGs. Players already buy, sell, and trade inside Discord servers and Facebook groups with no structure behind it. Nanza lets them keep trading with their friends and local groups, in a product designed around their needs. It's free, members pay to raise their limits, and there's no commission on trades.
>
> We're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios. Next year we're adding a small number of games, and [Game] is one we keep coming back to — [one specific, honest reason: the community, the art, how it played at Gen Con].
>
> Would you have thirty minutes in the next couple of weeks? I would love to learn more about your community.
>
> [Name]
> [Role], Nanza
> [email] · nanza.app

**Notes.** Name one real thing about the game every time; a generic intro to a founder reads as spam. If they're active in their own Discord, that's usually the fastest route. If a founder replies with anything beyond "sure, when?" — post it in Slack.

## Group 2 — Licensor-backed TCGs

The games owned by corporate licensors and publishers: Pokémon, Bandai (One Piece, Digimon, Dragon Ball), Disney and Ravensburger (Lorcana), Hasbro and Wizards, Konami, Bushiroad, Asmodee, and the rest of the licensor list. These are the biggest names in the hobby, and the people who own their community, organized play, and digital decisions sit inside large organizations. We go in through LinkedIn first, a connection note and then a message, and by email when we have it. The tone is professional, direct, and specific about the game and the team we want to reach. The ask is thirty minutes with the decision makers; the other two tiers exist to get us to them.

Three tiers, three slightly different asks. Tier B is the target: executives are too senior to take the call themselves, and team members can't make the decision. Tiers A and C exist only to get us to Tier B. Use the connection map to see which tier we already have at each company.

### Tier A — Executives (CEO, president, GM, C-suite)

Goal: get routed, never the call itself. A CEO or president is too senior for a thirty-minute conversation about community trading; the win is a forward to whoever owns community, organized play, or digital. Make forwarding easy by naming the game and the function.

**LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars)**

> Hi [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza, a social platform for TCG players (free, no commission on trades). We're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios. Would like to connect ahead of a note about [Game].

**LinkedIn message / email**

**Subject:** Nanza — 30 minutes with the [Game] team

> Hi [Recipient name],
>
> I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza. Nanza is a social platform built for TCGs. Players already buy, sell, and trade inside Discord servers and Facebook groups with no structure behind it. Nanza lets them keep trading with their friends and local groups, in a product designed around their needs. The business model is similar to Discord: the product is free and members pay to raise their limits. There is no commission on trades.
>
> We're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios, so we can share real usage rather than a concept. [Game] is the closest fit for what we're building next.
>
> I'm not looking for a partnership conversation yet. I'd like thirty minutes with whoever owns community and organized play for [Game], and whoever owns digital, mainly to learn more about community trading at [Company] and the data behind it. If you can point me to the right people, I'll take it from there.
>
> Thank you,
> [Name]
> [Role], Nanza
> [email] · nanza.app

### Tier B — Decision makers (VPs, directors, department heads)

Goal: the call itself. These are the people who own community, organized play, brand, licensing, or digital publishing for the game. Lead with their function, not the company.

**LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars)**

I'd like thirty minutes with you to learn more about community trading on the [Game] [community / organized play / digital] team and the data behind it, and to share what we're seeing with Flesh and Blood. ### Tier C — Team members (managers, coordinators, individual contributors)

Goal: a meeting with their boss. These contacts can't make the decision, but they know who does and often reply faster. Keep it lighter, and ask for the introduction to the person who runs their team.

**LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars)**

> Hi [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza, a social platform for TCG players. Fellow [Game] person here; we're live with Flesh and Blood today. I would love to connect.

**LinkedIn message**

> Hi [Recipient name] — thanks for connecting. Quick context: Nanza is a social platform built for TCGs — the trading players already do in Discord and Facebook groups, in a product made for it. Free, no commission on trades. We're live with Flesh and Blood and have a relationship with Legend Story Studios.
>
> I'd like thirty minutes with whoever runs [community / organized play / digital] for [Game] to talk about community trading and share what we're seeing with Flesh and Blood. Would you be open to introducing me to the person who leads your team, or letting me know who that is so I can reach out directly?

**Notes.** Tier B is the target; Tiers A and C are routes to it. Send Tier A and Tier B at the same company in parallel; don't wait on the executive to route. When a Tier C contact names someone, that name goes into the connection map before anyone reaches out. Any reply that contains a question goes in Slack.

## Group 3 — Investors

The people who could fund the seed round: VCs, especially gaming-focused funds, angels, seed funds, and friends and family. Warm intros come first, LinkedIn when we don't have one, and email for friends and family. The tone is confident and brief: what Nanza is, one proof point, one line on what's next, and what we want from them. The ask is an introductory call with the founders. Never quote round size, valuation, or terms in an intro; that's a Slack conversation first (see [[investor-relations-questionnaire]]).

## Group 4 — Affiliates and vendors

Affiliates are the content creators, streamers, and community voices players already trust. Vendors are the stores and sellers who supply that community. Together they're how a game's community comes onto Nanza, one game at a time, starting with Flesh and Blood. We reach them wherever they publish, YouTube, Discord, X, and Instagram DMs, and then by email. The tone is warm, specific to their work, and concrete about what's in it for them. The ask is a short call, or for affiliates, the October pilot: three paid posts and honest feedback.

One intro per game community. Swap the game, the platform, and the reason.

### Affiliate — DM

> Hey [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza. We're building the social feed for [Game]: follow the heroes, classes, and cards you care about and the feed fills with the posts, listings, and people around them. Free to use, no commission on trades, and members pay for higher limits, like Discord.
>
> We're bringing on a small group of [Game] creators as affiliates: home-page placement, no usage caps, and you get paid when people join through your link and when you feature vendor listings in your posts. I've been watching [channel / series / specific piece] for a while and you're exactly who we want in that group. Got fifteen minutes this week?

## Group 5 — Community members

The regular players, collectors, and traders: the people posting in Discord servers, Facebook groups, and subreddits, running local trade nights, and showing up at the game's events. They are the product's actual users, and their honesty is worth more than any call. We reach them by Discord, Facebook, and Reddit DMs, in person at events, and in replies to their public posts where the group allows it. The tone is the most casual of the five groups, one player to another, with no titles and no company talk. The ask is to try Nanza and tell us what's wrong with it.

Respect group rules. Never mass-post in a server or group; a moderator ban costs more than any single signup. If a group has a self-promotion channel, use it once and well.

### Discord / Facebook / Reddit DM

> Hey [Recipient name] — I'm [Name], [Role] at Nanza, a social app for [Game] players. It's the trading you already do in [this server / this group], in an app made for it: follow the cards and heroes you care about, see listings and posts from people you trust, message before you deal. Free, no commission on trades.
>
> Saw your [trade post / collection / deck] and figured you'd have opinions. Want to try it and tell me what we got wrong? I'll get you set up.

### Reply to a public trade post (where allowed)

> Nice [card / lot]. If you're doing a lot of these, we're building Nanza for exactly this — trading with the people you already know, free, no commission. Happy to get you in early if you want to try it.

### In person (events, trade nights)

Say the short blurb, then ask what they use to trade today and what annoys them about it. Get a handle or email; send the DM above afterward so there's a written intro they can share.

**Notes.** Community intros never mention licensors, investors, or the roadmap. If a community member turns out to be a moderator, a store owner, or a creator, they belong in Group 4 or the connection map; post it in Slack before continuing.
